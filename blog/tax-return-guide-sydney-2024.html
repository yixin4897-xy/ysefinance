Complete Tax Return Guide for Sydney 2025–2026

A practical FY2025–26 guide for Sydney employees, investors and sole traders. Check the rules for 1 July 2025 to 30 June 2026, organise your records and understand what needs individual advice.

Updated 27 October 2026 · FY2025–26 (1 July 2025–30 June 2026). 阅读简体中文版.

Need to get ready before calculating tax? Start with our 2026 preparation guide: documents and pre-fill checks. This article is the detailed FY2025–26 tax reference.

Key Dates & Lodgment Deadlines

Lodge your 2025–26 return after 30 June 2026. Wait until employer income statements are marked “tax ready” and reconcile bank, investment and other pre-fill information; pre-fill may be incomplete. If lodging yourself, the usual 31 October deadline falls on Saturday in 2026, so the next-business-day rule makes the deadline Monday 2 November 2026.

Engage a registered tax agent before 31 October 2026 if you want to use the agent lodgment program. Later dates depend on your eligibility, lodgment history and the ATO date allocated to your return. Overdue returns or other circumstances can mean an earlier date; a May deadline is not automatic. Ask your agent to confirm your exact lodgment and payment dates. An extension to lodge does not necessarily extend payment time.

Sources: ATO lodgment timing and tax agent lodgment arrangements; see also the next-business-day rule.

Australian Resident Income Tax Rates for 2025–26

These full-year resident rates apply to taxable income, before offsets, Medicare and HELP. Part-year residents, foreign residents, working holiday makers and some minors have different rules.

The 16% band remains in place for 2025–26. The legislated reduction to 15% starts on 1 July 2026 (2026–27); do not apply it to this return. Sources: ATO resident tax rates and effective dates of the tax cuts.

Medicare Levy and Medicare Levy Surcharge

The ordinary Medicare levy is generally 2% of taxable income, subject to reductions and exemptions. For 2025–26, the ordinary individual low-income threshold is $28,011, with phase-in up to $35,013. For individuals entitled to the seniors and pensioners tax offset (SAPTO), the corresponding thresholds are $44,268 and $55,335; age alone does not establish entitlement.

The family low-income threshold is $47,238, or $61,623 where the SAPTO family rules apply, plus $4,338 for each dependent child or student. Individual income, combined family taxable income and dependant rules affect the calculation; this is not a blanket exemption for every member of a household. These increased thresholds are enacted for 2025–26 assessments: see Schedule 5, including its application provision, and the ATO levy reduction guidance.

The Medicare levy surcharge (MLS) is separate. If you, your spouse or dependants lack appropriate private patient hospital cover, the following 2025–26 income tiers may apply for uncovered days. Extras-only cover does not qualify, and buying cover now cannot remove liability for earlier uncovered days.

MLS income includes specified additions such as reportable fringe benefits, reportable super contributions and net investment losses; it is not necessarily taxable income. Family income selects the tier, while each person's liability is assessed separately on the applicable MLS base and days, with exemptions considered. Private hospital insurance does not itself remove the ordinary 2% levy.

Sources: ATO MLS income definitions and rates and Australian Government hospital-cover and family rules. Use the 2025–26 tiers, not the higher 2026–27 thresholds.

HELP / HECS: New Marginal Repayments

From 1 July 2025, compulsory study-loan repayments use a new $67,000 threshold and marginal calculation, with a whole-income cap at the top tier. Repayment income includes taxable income plus net investment losses, reportable fringe benefits, reportable super contributions and exempt foreign employment income.

Example: at $80,000 repayment income, 15% × ($80,000 − $67,000) = $1,950, assuming enough debt remains. This is separate from income tax and Medicare. Salary sacrifice and rental losses can affect repayment income differently from taxable income.

The enacted 20% reduction of eligible student-loan balances at 1 June 2025 is a separate debt-account measure, not an extra deduction, refund promise or change to this repayment formula. Check your loan account and PAYG withholding. Sources: ATO repayment thresholds and student-loan law changes.

Work-Related Deductions: Eligibility Before Amounts

You must have incurred the expense yourself, have a direct connection to earning assessable income, and keep the required evidence. Exclude employer-reimbursed costs and apportion mixed expenses to work use. An allowance does not automatically make an expense deductible. Ordinary clothes, normal meals and private living costs are not deductible simply because you work.

Eligible protective or occupation-specific clothing, professional memberships, tools and education connected to your current income-earning activities may qualify, subject to their own rules. Keep receipts, invoices and a calculation of work use. The limited $300 substantiation exception is not an automatic $300 deduction: you still need actual eligible spending and a basis for the calculation. ATO deductions and substantiation rules.

Working From Home: 70¢ per Hour or Actual Costs

For eligible 2025–26 work from home, the fixed-rate method is 70¢ per hour. It covers energy, internet/data, mobile and home phone usage, stationery and computer consumables. Keep a contemporaneous record of every actual hour throughout the year, not a reconstructed estimate or sample week, and at least one bill or receipt for each covered expense type you incurred.

Do not separately claim costs included in the rate, including work phone/internet usage away from home. Eligible work-related decline in value of equipment may be separate, with purchase and usage evidence. Alternatively, use the actual-cost method with records of the additional expenses and reasonable work/private apportionment; do not claim the same costs under both methods. Employees generally cannot claim home rent, mortgage interest or rates merely because they work from home.

Example: 600 properly recorded eligible hours × $0.70 = $420 for the covered costs, not $420 plus the same internet and electricity bills. Source: ATO PCG 2023/1, fixed-rate and recordkeeping requirements.

Car Expenses: 88¢ per Eligible Kilometre

The cents-per-kilometre method uses 88¢ for 2025–26, capped at 5,000 eligible work/business kilometres per car per year. Show how you calculated kilometres using trip records. This rate includes fuel, servicing, insurance, registration and decline in value: do not claim those costs again. Ordinary home-to-regular-work commuting is generally private, even if you work late; limited exceptions need evidence.

The logbook method instead uses actual expenses and a supported business-use percentage. Keep a representative continuous 12-week logbook, odometer records and expense evidence; a logbook can generally support up to five years if usage remains representative. Different rules apply to vehicles outside the tax definition of a car. Example: 2,000 eligible kilometres × $0.88 = $1,760, not a tax refund of $1,760. Sources: ATO car-expense methods and ATO confirmation of the 2025–26 rate.

Rental Properties: Interest, Repairs and Capital Costs

Declare gross rent and keep agent statements and invoices. Deductible expenses depend on income-producing use and genuine availability for rent; apportion private stays, below-market arrangements and ownership shares. Loan interest follows how borrowed funds are used, not which property secures the loan. A private redraw is not deductible just because it sits in an investment loan. Principal repayments are not interest deductions.

Example: for an unchanged $500,000 borrowing used $400,000 for a rental and $100,000 privately, only 80% of the interest is potentially deductible, subject to the other conditions. Mixed loans with changing balances require transaction-level records.

A repair restoring damage from rental use may be immediately deductible. Initial repairs for defects present at purchase, improvements and replacement of an entire asset are capital, not ordinary repair deductions. Division 40 covers eligible depreciating assets such as appliances, with restrictions on many second-hand residential rental assets. Division 43 covers eligible building/structural capital works, often at 2.5% annually over 40 years; construction dates, costs and eligibility matter.

Capital works deductions claimed or claimable generally reduce the property’s CGT cost base where the rules require; you cannot both deduct expenditure and include it again in cost base. Keep construction schedules and acquisition/disposal records. Sources: ATO capital works and CGT guidance and interest and redraw ruling TR 2000/2.

Shares, ETFs, Crypto and Overseas Investments

Report interest, dividends and eligible franking credits using statements, and reconcile managed-fund/ETF tax statements (including AMMA statements). Reinvesting a distribution does not make it tax-free; attributed taxable amounts can differ from cash received. Retain cost-base adjustments and foreign ETF records.

Selling investments, gifting them or swapping crypto can trigger CGT even without withdrawing cash. Capital losses generally offset capital gains, not salary; unused net capital losses carry forward. Eligible resident individuals may receive a 50% CGT discount after holding an asset for at least 12 months, after applying capital losses. Trading as a business can produce different treatment.

Example: an eligible resident investor has a $6,000 share gain and a $2,000 capital loss. If the gain qualifies for the 50% discount, ($6,000 − $2,000) × 50% = $2,000 net capital gain, assuming no other gains/losses. Crypto rewards or business receipts may also be ordinary income; disposal later can have separate consequences.

Sources: ATO CGT, ETFs and crypto assets.

Tax Residency, Overseas Income and AUD Conversion

Australian tax residency is determined under tax tests, not by citizenship, visa or the location of your bank alone. Residents generally report worldwide income, including overseas wages, rent, interest, dividends and taxable gains, even if retained offshore or taxed overseas. Special temporary-resident exemptions, foreign-resident rules, part-year residency and tax treaties can change the outcome. Working holiday makers have separate rules; foreign residents generally have no resident tax-free threshold or Medicare levy.

Translate foreign income, expenses, foreign tax and investment acquisition/disposal amounts to AUD under the applicable conversion rules. Generally use the relevant transaction-date rate; an average is allowed only where appropriate. Keep dates, original amounts, rate sources and calculations; foreign calendar-year statements may need splitting across Australia’s July–June year.

A foreign income tax offset may be available for qualifying foreign tax paid on amounts included in Australian assessable income. It is not automatically a dollar-for-dollar refund. An offset claim above $1,000 requires a limit calculation; unused offset is not refundable or carried forward. Sources: ATO residency, foreign income and exchange rates.

Sole Traders and Side Hustles

Include assessable business receipts, including cash, platform and relevant non-cash payments. A hobby/business assessment depends on facts; having no ABN or earning less than the GST threshold does not make assessable income tax-free. An ABN identifies an eligible enterprise; it is not GST registration and does not by itself determine whether you are an employee or contractor.

GST registration is generally required once current or projected GST turnover reaches $75,000 ($150,000 for non-profit bodies). The turnover test concerns sales, not profit, and applies across your enterprises, with statutory exclusions. Taxi and ride-sourcing passenger services generally require registration regardless of turnover. Register within 21 days when required. Keep sales records, invoices, expense evidence, business-use calculations and BAS reconciliations; GST credits and income-tax deductions must not double count GST. Capital assets and business losses have additional rules. ATO GST registration.

2025–26 Tax Return Checklist for Sydney Residents

  • Check residency dates, spouse/dependant details, bank account details and private hospital-cover days.
  • Reconcile tax-ready income statements, allowances, bank interest, dividends and ETF/managed-fund tax statements.
  • Collect rental income, loan-use evidence, repair invoices and Division 40/43 schedules.
  • Export share and crypto transactions, capital losses and overseas income/tax records with AUD conversions.
  • Gather eligible expense receipts, full-year home-working hours and car trip/logbook records; remove private and reimbursed amounts.
  • Reconcile sole-trader sales, business expenses, GST/BAS and PAYG instalments; check HELP repayment income.
  • Check any offset or personal super deduction separately, including eligibility, contribution caps and a valid acknowledged notice of intent where required.
  • Confirm the actual lodgment/payment dates; retain the lodged return, assessment and supporting calculations.

Keep individual tax records generally for five years from lodgment; longer periods can apply to assets, carried-forward losses and disputes. Keep acquisition records through ownership and the required period after disposal, and depreciation records through the required period after the last claim. Business retention rules can differ. Digital copies must be legible and accessible. ATO recordkeeping.

Common mistakes include trusting incomplete pre-fill, claiming a standard amount without spending, duplicating fixed-rate costs, deducting commuting, ignoring crypto swaps or foreign income, and assuming an agent always gives an extension. A deduction reduces taxable income; it is not a guaranteed reimbursement.
