How resilient is Australia's economy?

The Australian Economic Sustainability Model (AESM) is an independent research framework examining households, credit, banking resilience and public finances through transparent official data.

What this research is

A framework, not a forecast.

AESM is being developed to examine whether Australian economic growth and financial obligations appear sustainable over time. It distinguishes vulnerability—exposure to economic shocks—from resilience—the capacity to absorb them.

The first proposed component, the Australian Self-Financing Index (ASFI), contains eight indicators in four dimensions. A separate Australian Economic Health Index (AEHI) is in the design stage. An overall AESI has not been launched.

03 / Methodology and evidence

Designed to be auditable

Quarterly review means examining newly published evidence each quarter—not pretending every source is available immediately on quarter-end.

Research process

  1. Collect and preserve source releases.Record the original observation period, source, release date, unit, and any statistical revision.
  2. Build a point-in-time dataset.Only incorporate observations after their public release date; preserve earlier versions.
  3. Assess each dimension separately.Test economic scoring assumptions against independent outcomes, not just historical percentiles.
  4. Validate before publication.Require scope comparability, full evidence, independent methodology review and editorial sign-off.

Current evidence limits

Research limitations: exact contemporaneous RBA E2/E13 readings for the historical 2025 Q4 test remain unverified. Some APRA readings are rounded or use an earlier quarter. Previous validation windows were short, overlapping and partly influenced by the pandemic.

The preliminary 0–100 piecewise scoring system uses researcher-selected thresholds. They are not official regulatory safety benchmarks and have not been validated as recession probabilities.

A score near any proposed alert line must not be treated as a forecast, investment signal or policy judgement.

Comparability and accounting decisions

Interest burden is not total repayments

S02 uses RBA Housing Interest Charged to Household Disposable Income. It is not the scheduled mortgage repayment ratio, which includes principal. RBA changed the relevant statistical publication in February 2023.

Bank regulation differs by institution

Capital and funding requirements depend on the applicable prudential framework. For applicable NSFR-regulated institutions, 100% is the regulatory minimum; research scoring anchors above it remain illustrative.

Accrual fiscal interest, not cash paid

S08 is defined as ABS General Government Interest Expenses n.e.c. divided by Total GFS Revenue for all government levels, excluding separately reported defined-benefit superannuation interest.

Annual data are not backdated

S07 and S08 refer to financial years. They enter any quarterly research snapshot only after their source release becomes public, and later ABS revisions are retained as separate versions.

Frequently asked questions

Why is there no published ASFI score?

Original-source verification, scope checks and independent validation are incomplete. A data-quality HOLD must not be mistaken for an adverse economic assessment.

Will AESM be updated quarterly?

The plan is a quarterly research review. Some inputs are published after quarter-end and ABS fiscal measures are annual, so any future public update will follow verified release dates.

Can AESM predict a recession?

No reliable forward predictive claim has been established. The earlier backtests are exploratory, use limited overlapping windows and include unusual pandemic periods.

Is AESM published by the RBA, ABS or APRA?

No. AESM is an independent YSE Finance research initiative that references public statistics issued by those agencies.
